    Stop 1 of 5

    Satellite and pasture

    What it measures. Overnight, an earth-observation satellite passed over the farm. Its camera measures how green and thick the grass is, which gives an estimate of pasture cover for every paddock, every few days when the sky is clear.

    How AI uses it. The AI helper compares this pass with last week's and with the feed plan, then lists the paddocks that are ahead or behind. It also notes how old each reading is.

    Question. Cloud has hidden the farm for three days. What should the brief do about pasture cover?

    • Use the last clear reading and say how old it is
    • Make up a figure from the weather
    • Leave pasture out and say nothing

    Answer: Use the last clear reading and say how old it is. An old number is still useful if the brief is honest about its age, so you know how much to trust it.

    Stop 2 of 5

    Soil probe and weather station

    What it measures. The soil probe logs moisture and temperature at several depths every 15 minutes. The weather station on the ridge logs rain, wind, temperature and humidity.

    How AI uses it. The helper reads them together: how wet the soil is, how much rain fell, and what's forecast. It turns that into one line, for example "soil drying on the flats, growth likely to slow by Thursday".

    Question. The probe says the soil is dry, but the station logged 20 mm of rain overnight. What should the brief do?

    • Trust the rain gauge and ignore the probe
    • Flag the mismatch so someone checks the sensors
    • Average the two and carry on

    Answer: Flag the mismatch so someone checks the sensors. When two sources disagree, the brief should say so. A blocked gauge or a probe that's shifted is worth a look.

    Stop 3 of 5

    Collars and a virtual fence

    What it measures. Each animal wears a collar that logs where it is and how much it moves and grazes. The virtual fence is a line drawn on a map. The collar uses sound and a gentle vibration to keep stock on the right side of it.

    How AI uses it. The helper watches for animals behaving differently from usual, such as grazing less for two days, and suggests when to open the next break of fresh grass.

    Question. The brief says one cow has been grazing less for two days. What happens next?

    • The collar treats the cow
    • The farmer checks the cow and decides
    • Wait until the data is certain

    Answer: The farmer checks the cow and decides. The data says where to look. A person looks at the animal and decides what to do.

    Stop 4 of 5

    Tractor and spray drone

    What it measures. The electric tractor started drilling on the river flats at four. It logs where it has been and how much seed went in. The spray drone has a job planned for this morning.

    How AI uses it. The helper checks the spray job against the wind forecast. The wind picks up after ten, so it flags the job amber: HOLD.

    Question. Gusts are forecast to rise above the limit on the spray label after ten. What should the brief do?

    • Cancel the job by itself
    • Suggest holding the job, and let the farmer decide
    • Spray faster before the wind arrives

    Answer: Suggest holding the job, and let the farmer decide. The helper suggests. The farmer decides, and can see the forecast behind the suggestion.

    Stop 5 of 5

    The morning brief

    What it measures. At the gate, everything from the night arrives as one short brief on the phone: feed, water, stock, jobs and paperwork.

    How AI uses it. Each line points back to where it came from: the satellite, the probe, the collars, the tractor log. Drafted records wait for a tick before they go anywhere.

    • Feed: Paddocks 7 and 12 ahead of plan. Paddock 3 behind. (source: Satellite, 2 days old)
    • Water: Soil drying on the flats. No action today. (source: Soil probe + weather station)
    • Stock: One cow grazing less for 2 days. Check her this morning. (source: Collars)
    • Jobs: Drilling on the flats 60% done. Spraying: HOLD, wind after 10. (source: Tractor log + forecast)
    • Records: 2 drafts ready for your tick. (source: Waiting for you)

    Question. One line in a brief has no source next to it. What do you do?

    • Use it. The other lines checked out
    • Check it before you act on it
    • Bin the whole brief

    Answer: Check it before you act on it. The same habit as the rest of this course: give AI a clear job, then check its answer.

    The farmer still makes the calls

    The technology does the reading. The farmer still makes the calls. Getting there starts smaller than a robot: give AI one clear job, and check its answer. That's what the rest of this lesson is about.
