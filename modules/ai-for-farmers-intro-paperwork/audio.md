## Full podcast script

Host: A tidy roster can hide an unavailable worker, and a short notice can hide an unconfirmed date. This module uses fictional Example Station notes to practise preparing drafts and checking them. Listen without looking at the screen. Pause for work that needs attention and return to the activity when parked.

Farmer: Our practice roster has Worker A available Monday and Tuesday. Worker B can work Tuesday and Wednesday. Each day needs a morning check.

Host: A can cover Monday, either worker can cover Tuesday and B can cover Wednesday. Read each assignment against availability. A draft placing A on Wednesday fails even if every day has someone in the table. The note says A is unavailable then.

Farmer: If B becomes unavailable on Wednesday too, I need the draft to show a gap. Moving A into the space would hide the problem.

Host: Ask the manager to arrange coverage. For a real roster, check agreed hours, breaks, leave, competence and coverage with the team. Use roles in public practice and keep personal leave reasons out of an AI chat. The helper does not need someone's medical information to show an uncovered day.

Farmer: A neighbour notice has another kind of gap. I might know the block and possible day while the operator still needs to check the plan.

Host: Our invented note proposes work in Practice Block on Friday. The exact date, time, hazards, affected parties and notification requirements remain unconfirmed. Ask for a preparation draft with visible blanks. The responsible operator checks the product label, applicable regional plan and GROWSAFE guidance before real use.

Farmer: A clearly written message still cannot approve spraying. I also need to know how a postponement would be communicated.

Host: Keep that question in the draft. Notification arrangements vary, so reject a blanket deadline supplied by the helper. Check recipients and attachments before sending a real notice. The course activity sends nothing and has no real neighbours or contact details.

Farmer: The same check applies to a contractor email. "Could Tuesday suit?" can become "See you Tuesday" while the helper shortens it.

Host: Compare every promise with the source. For a vet enquiry, report observations and ask what information is needed. Leave diagnosis and treatment with the vet. Urgent concerns go directly through the farm's animal-health arrangements.

Farmer: Record summaries worry me too. Someone marks maintenance as planned, then a summary calls it complete.

Host: Restore the source status and ask for completion evidence. Our fictional movement card says official confirmation has not been checked. That gap must stay visible. An equipment activity log also cannot prove the required inspection happened or that legal conditions were met.

Farmer: A draft induction could make up a meeting place when none is listed. That would look helpful until somebody relied on it.

Host: Keep the supplied sign-in and waiting instructions. Ask the supervisor for current emergency arrangements. The responsible person checks actual hazards and approved procedures before a real induction is used. Shortening must preserve conditions.

Farmer: When parked, I will build the notice prompt, remove a date chip and see what the draft loses. Then I will review the gaps.

Host: Try the activity and the quiz using fictional material. Keep original notes separate from checked drafts so someone can follow the status and evidence. AI drafts; the responsible person decides whether the draft is ready to use.

## Recording notes

547 spoken words excluding speaker labels, about 4.6 minutes at 120 words per minute. Record calm NZ English with distinct voices and brief pauses at speaker changes. Keep total runtime within 4-6 minutes. No music or machinery sounds beneath speech. Do not add advice or require a screen during listening. Read numbers and units in full as written.
