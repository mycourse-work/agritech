## Full podcast script

Host: You have a gate note and want a contractor enquiry before another job takes your attention. An AI helper can turn a short note into a readable draft. This listening version uses fictional Example Station material. You can listen without looking at a screen and do the activity when parked. Pause whenever the work around you needs your attention.

Farmer: My note says the hinge needs inspection. Tuesday would suit us, but I have not booked anyone. I would want the helper to keep that distinction.

Host: Give it the note, the job and the reader. Ask for a short enquiry about availability. Add a limit: do not invent a time or promise. That request is a prompt. You can write it, dictate it while parked or complete the exercise on paper.

Farmer: Suppose it writes, "See you at eight on Tuesday." It sounds like a normal email. I might skim past it.

Host: Compare it with the note before using it. Eight is absent, and Tuesday is a request. The draft has turned uncertainty into a commitment. Cross out the invented time and ask whether Tuesday suits. The corrected message still needs another source check.

Farmer: So I keep my rough note beside the answer. I do not ask the same helper whether its answer is true and call that a check.

Host: A repeated answer is another draft. Read the evidence yourself. Check dates, names, quantities and promises. Recalculate a number that affects the job. Open a cited source and read the words that support the claim. A link title alone cannot show that an answer is correct.

Farmer: What if the source is a scan and the line is blurred? The helper may still give me a number.

Host: Preserve the gap and obtain a readable original. Ask it to say unknown. An instruction to avoid guessing helps define the job, but it cannot guarantee the reply follows that instruction. Your comparison with the source is still needed.

Farmer: I could start with shortening a message because I can check the whole thing quickly. I would be less confident checking advice about a sick animal.

Host: Choose a small task you can verify. Animal health, spraying and legal decisions need the responsible person and relevant evidence. If an animal concern is urgent, follow the farm's arrangements and seek the vet directly. Do not wait for a chat answer to decide what to do.

Farmer: And voice input could introduce its own mistake before the helper even answers. A tractor is hardly a quiet recording room.

Host: Practise while parked. Read the transcript before submitting it, especially numbers and dates. Avoid recording other people's conversations. In this course the activities contain invented material and run locally; an AI account is unnecessary.

Farmer: When I am parked, I will compare the draft and the original note, mark any added detail and read the explanation. I can make the correction myself.

Host: That is the module's practice task. Try the made-up-fact activity, then the scenario quiz. Keep the original evidence and the revised draft together so another person can see why you changed the wording.

## Recording notes

511 spoken words excluding speaker labels, about 4.3 minutes at 120 words per minute. Record calm NZ English with distinct voices and brief pauses at speaker changes. Keep total runtime within 4-6 minutes. No music or machinery sounds beneath speech. Do not add advice or require a screen during listening. Read numbers and units in full as written.
