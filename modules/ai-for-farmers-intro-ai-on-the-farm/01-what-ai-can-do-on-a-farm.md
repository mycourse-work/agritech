It's nine at night in the second week of calving. A relief milker starts Monday and your notes on the shed routine are half a page of scribble. An AI chat tool can turn them into a clear message in a couple of minutes.

> **On the farm:** A lot of farm admin is writing: texts to staff, rosters, procedures, meeting notes. AI tools are quick at first drafts of all of it. The skill is knowing which jobs to hand over and which information to keep to yourself.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Name the main AI chat tools and describe what they do.
- Pick out farm jobs that suit an AI tool and jobs that don't.
- Explain why a chat tool can't tell you today's schedule price or your pasture cover.
- Decide what information stays out of the chat.

## What these tools are

ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini and Claude are AI chat tools. You type or speak a request in plain English and they write a reply, on your phone or computer. For everyday jobs they're much of a muchness, so use whichever one you already have.

They learned from huge amounts of text, so their writing reads well. But they don't know your farm. They know their training and whatever you put in the chat. DairyNZ's page [Using Artificial Intelligence on-farm](https://www.dairynz.co.nz/people/productive-workplaces/using-artificial-intelligence-on-farm/) covers how farmers are using them, limits included.

The examples in this course come from Ridgeback Farm, which is made up.

## Six jobs they handle well

| Job | What you give it | What you get back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staff messages and translations | Rough notes for a new starter | A clear message, plus a version in their first language |
| Rosters from notes | Who can work which days, from a text thread | A draft roster table to check |
| Summarising a long document | A resource consent or grazing contract | A one-page summary of conditions and dates |
| Standard operating procedures | How you wash down the yard, spoken into your phone | A numbered procedure for the dairy wall |
| Meeting notes | Your scribbles from the team meeting | Actions with names and days |
| Explaining a report | A herd test report or soil test results | Plain-English terms, plus questions for your vet or adviser |

In every one, you supply the facts and you read the result before it goes anywhere.

### Worked example: a welcome message

Here's what you might type for the new relief milker:

```text
I manage a dairy farm in New Zealand. Turn these notes into a short,
friendly text for Jun, a relief milker starting Monday. Then give me
the same message in Tagalog.

Notes: 4.30 am Monday, meet me at the dairy. Bring own gumboots, we
supply overalls and gloves. Hannah will show you cups-on and the
plant wash. Ring me if running late.
```

A good answer looks like this, followed by the Tagalog version:

```text
Hi Jun, welcome to the team! For Monday:

- Start 4.30 am at the dairy. I'll meet you there.
- Bring your own gumboots. We supply overalls and gloves.
- Hannah will show you cups-on and the plant wash.
- If you're running late, give me a ring.
```

Every fact came from your notes and nothing was added. Translations can trip on farm words like "cups-on", so ask Jun to say if anything's unclear.

## What they're bad at

- **Current prices.** Ask for this week's prime steer schedule and you may get an old or overseas figure, stated with full confidence. Get prices from your processor or agent.
- **Your farm's live data.** The tool can't see your pasture cover, herd records or rain gauge, so "how much cover have I got?" gets a guess. Most chat tools don't connect to farm software, as DairyNZ points out, so you paste the figures in yourself.
- **Anything you didn't give it.** If your notes don't say when the contractor is coming, a draft that names a time has made one up.
- **Calls that need a professional.** Animal health, spraying and legal questions go to your vet, spray contractor or adviser.

## What stays out of the chat

The [Privacy Commissioner](https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ai/) is clear that the Privacy Act applies to everyone using AI tools in New Zealand, and your staff's details are personal information. Keep these out:

- Staff IRD numbers, bank accounts, addresses, medical certificates, visa details and disciplinary notes.
- Your own bank and card numbers, passwords and banking codes.
- Other people's information, such as grazing clients' records or anything a buyer gave you in confidence.

Use first names or roles ("the new relief milker"). DairyNZ notes that free versions may use what you type, so check the tool's data settings.

## Key takeaways

- ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Claude all handle everyday farm writing. Use the one you've got.
- They're quick at staff messages, translations, rosters, summaries, procedures and meeting notes.
- They only know their training and what you give them, so they can't tell you today's prices or your cover.
- A detail you didn't supply is a guess, however sure it sounds.
- Keep staff personal details, bank details and other people's information out of the chat.

Start with the writing job you dodge every week. Let the tool do the first draft, then read it and send it yourself, because it goes out with your name on it.
