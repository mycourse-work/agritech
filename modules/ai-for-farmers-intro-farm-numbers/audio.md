## Full podcast script

Host: This module asks what a number represents before asking what to do with it. All quantities belong to invented Example Station exercises. Listen without looking at the screen and pause whenever your work needs attention. The maths, documents and drift activity can wait until you are parked.

Farmer: I have ten millimetres of observed rain yesterday and a forecast of twenty tomorrow. It would be easy for a summary to say thirty millimetres received.

Host: That mixes a measurement with a forecast. Keep ten observed and twenty forecast, each with its period. Today's reading is missing. A blank is unknown; it must not become zero. A future forecast also needs its issue time and current conditions before real planning.

Farmer: What about the feed worksheet? I want to understand the arithmetic without being told the result proves enough feed.

Host: Our invented sheet starts with one thousand kilograms of dry matter, adds one hundred and subtracts five hundred of expected use. That gives six hundred kilograms of dry matter. Recalculate it yourself. Then check the inputs and assumptions. Dry matter and fresh weight describe different quantities. The sum cannot establish a suitable real feed plan.

Farmer: A document has similar traps. Two clear quantities might add up while another row is unreadable.

Host: The fictional packing extract has twenty pieces and thirty pieces, plus a blurred quantity. Fifty is the clear-row subtotal. The document total is unknown. Ask for a readable original and compare every extracted value with its exact row. Never borrow a missing figure from an older invoice.

Farmer: If I ask about a real kill sheet, I need permission to use it and the processor's definitions. A plausible explanation of a result could still be wrong.

Host: Check the original headings, date, identifiers, units and whether figures are totals or averages. List unclear entries as questions for the processor. This public course uses no real processor results. A weighing location also does not establish grazing history without movement records.

Farmer: Bringing app exports together adds more questions. Similar location names and repeated rows are easy to overlook.

Host: Keep original exports and an index of dates, periods, scope and units. Propose uncertain name matches for review. Check event identity and origin before removing a repeated row from a working copy. A sensor spike needs investigation and a missing week cannot establish a trend.

Farmer: Before uploading files I should check sharing rights too. An export button does not tell me who may reuse a derived summary.

Host: Ask about provider terms, permissions, storage, model training, retention and deletion. Entered data and derived information may have different rights. Use invented documents for public practice and keep login information out of chats.

Farmer: The drift picture is another output to question. I can change wind and droplets, but I cannot treat the plume as a field forecast.

Host: Correct. Its comparison begins at an illustrative fifteen kilometres an hour, not a legal limit. The model is stylised and supplies no operational approval. Check your regional plan, product label and GROWSAFE guidance for actual work. Operator checks include conditions the picture cannot establish.

Farmer: When parked, I will change one input and describe what the visual shows, then list what evidence is still missing.

Host: Complete the activity and quiz with that separation in mind. Keep the source, the assumptions and the unresolved questions beside any useful summary.

Keep total runtime within 4-6 minutes. No music or machinery sounds beneath speech. Do not add advice or require a screen during listening. Read numbers and units in full as written.
