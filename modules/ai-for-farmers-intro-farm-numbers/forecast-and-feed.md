# Separate a forecast from a measurement

![Reading your farm's numbers: generic farmer and teaching materials.](/api/content/ai-for-farmers-intro/@modules/ai-for-farmers-intro-farm-numbers/assets/ai-for-farmers-intro-farm-numbers-hero.jpg)

Allow 3 minutes. All Example Station details are invented.

A forecast describes what may happen. An observation records what was measured. Keep the labels and issue times when AI summarises both.

Fictional Example Station has a card showing observed rain of 10 mm yesterday and another showing forecast rain of 20 mm tomorrow. There is no observation for today. A summary of "30 mm received" is wrong. The observed amount is 10 mm, the forecast is 20 mm, and today's reading is unknown.

```text
Summarise these fictional rain cards. Separate observations,
forecasts and missing readings. Keep their periods visible.
List what needs checking before a planning decision.
```

## Check a feed sum yourself

An invented worksheet starts with 1,000 kg of dry matter available, adds 100 kg and subtracts 500 kg of expected use. The arithmetic result is 600 kg of dry matter. Recalculate it on a calculator. Dry matter describes feed with its water removed; keep it separate from fresh weight.

The sum says nothing about whether the starting estimate, added feed or expected use is accurate. Ask which quantities were measured and which were assumed. Changing an assumption produces a different scenario. It does not prove feed will be sufficient for actual animals.

A useful request asks for the arithmetic, units, assumptions and gaps separately. Get the responsible farm adviser or manager to check a real feed plan. A local pasture estimate and a whole-farm model also need matching periods, scope and methods before comparison.
